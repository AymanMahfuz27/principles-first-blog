# ChatGPT Pulse and the possibility of AI media

I think ChatGPT Pulse could become a useful personalized briefing, and that format raises a business-model question. A daily product can create recurring attention without requiring a user to begin every interaction with a prompt. Advertising might support that experience, but only if it does not compromise the information people came to read. That is a product hypothesis, not a claim about OpenAI's announced revenue plans.

I have thought about this since starting Inkwell. The idea there was to make books free through advertising, with revenue going to authors. The lesson I took from it was that a product's distribution and payment model can determine who gets to use it. A technically useful product still needs a way to pay for delivering it.

AI makes that question difficult because serving a model has ongoing costs. Subscriptions and API usage are possible answers. I do not know the economics well enough to claim they can never cover those costs, but I am interested in other formats that could make the product valuable to more people. A briefing is one such format.

## The assistant I was trying to build

I have wanted an assistant that prepares information before I ask for it. The Jarvis morning-briefing scenes in Iron Man were an early reference for that idea. I wanted to wake up with my schedule, relevant news, sports, and research already organized.

When scheduled tasks became available, I used them to build a morning newspaper for myself. It was awkward to configure, but it gave me a daily update without making me repeat the same request. The useful part was the persistent intent: I had stated what mattered once, and the system returned to it.

[Pulse's launch](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-pulse/) moves toward that experience with daily personalized briefings and optional connections to context such as email and calendar. The appeal is preparation. A good briefing reduces the work needed to find out what requires attention and what I want to learn about that day.

## Why the newspaper comparison fits

A newspaper combines editorial selection, reporting, recurring sections, and advertising. Personalization could make those sections more relevant: a paper I should read for my research, a development in a topic I follow, or an update related to my schedule. It could also make the briefing finite, with a clear point at which I have read what I need.

That is the product I would want Pulse to become. The available context can help it choose material, but relevance is not just similarity to my past behavior. It should also reflect my stated goals and distinguish an important update from something that merely attracts a click. Otherwise personalization can reproduce the same distractions as an ordinary feed.

Advertising would introduce a separate objective. A placement could be useful if it relates to a real need, but it should be clearly identified and separated from the briefing's factual claims. Embedding a commercial incentive into an answer makes it harder to tell whether the recommendation serves the user or the advertiser. That ambiguity is why ads in generated responses have felt wrong to me.

I can imagine a briefing with a limited number of visible ad placements, similar to a newspaper. Whether that would fund the product depends on usage, revenue, serving costs, and user tolerance. I cannot infer those numbers from the interface. My prediction is that the format creates an opportunity worth testing, rather than that advertising must become the business model.

## Curation and the feed incentive

[Meta's Vibes](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/09/introducing-vibes-ai-videos/amp/), a feed of short AI-generated videos, suggests a different direction for AI media. My concern is that making content cheaper to generate can increase the amount people consume without increasing its value. A product optimized for continuous scrolling has little reason to tell the user that they are finished.

Pulse has an opportunity to be judged by a different outcome: whether it leaves me informed and ready to do something useful. That outcome is compatible with a daily habit, but it can conflict with maximizing time spent in the app. The revenue model and product metrics would need to preserve the distinction.

The memory and context layer matters because it can make curation more useful over time. It also makes user control important. I would want to inspect why a topic appears, remove an unwanted topic, and change the goals that shape the briefing. A product with access to personal context should make those choices understandable.

What interests me is the reuse of a familiar format. AI does not have to invent a new category of activity to be useful. It can improve the newspaper by preparing it around one person's needs. Pulse's opportunity is to make that preparation reliable enough that the user spends less time gathering information and more time using it.
